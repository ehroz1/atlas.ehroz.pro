A 2019 website usually gives itself away on the first screen: a stock photo or a flat illustration of long-limbed people, a centred headline, three feature icons underneath. A 2026 one more often opens with big type, the real product interface and motion that explains something. Here are seven differences I see when browsing galleries, and where each is easiest to study.

These are observations, not rules: a good 2019 site can work better than a fashionable one. But if a site is due for a refresh, this list is a place to start.

## 1. The product instead of illustrations

**Then:** flat illustrations of long-armed people, abstract clouds and cogs.
**Now:** real screenshots and pieces of the interface, often live: buttons press, charts move.

[Saaspo](/tool/saaspo/) is a good place to see how software companies do this: compare first screens and pricing pages side by side.

![Saaspo: software company sites by section and page type](shot:saaspo)

## 2. Type became the hero

**Then:** a medium headline, with the picture doing the work.
**Now:** a very large headline across the screen, variable fonts, sometimes a first screen that is a single sentence.

Sites where type holds the layout often show up on [Minimal Gallery](/tool/minimal-gallery/) and [Siteinspire](/tool/siteinspire/): filter by site type and look at the first screen.

## 3. Motion explains instead of decorating

**Then:** "fade in on scroll" on every block.
**Now:** motion that shows how the product works: blocks assemble into an interface, text changes as you scroll, smooth transitions between pages.

A static screenshot can't show that. [Godly](/tool/godly/) and [landinglove](/tool/landing-love/) record sites as video, so you see the motion itself.

![Godly: sites as short videos, so the motion is visible](shot:godly)

## 4. Dark themes aren't the exception

**Then:** white by default, dark as a rare "stylish" option.
**Now:** many products have a dark site or a theme switch, and handling contrast and accents on black is a skill of its own.

[Dark Design](/tool/dark-design/) collects good examples: look at how the accent colour and images are chosen for a dark background.

## 5. Bento grids instead of three columns

**Then:** three equal columns of icon, heading and text.
**Now:** blocks of different sizes, like the compartments of a bento box, each with its own small example: a screenshot, a number, an animation.

Look for these on [Saaspo](/tool/saaspo/) and [Minimal Gallery](/tool/minimal-gallery/): bento grids are especially common on product and app sites.

## 6. The header got compact

**Then:** a wide white bar with the logo on the left and six menu items on the right.
**Now:** a small floating pill over the content, mega menus with pictures, a bar that hides as you scroll.

[Navbar Gallery](/tool/navbar-gallery/) is a whole gallery of these: headers, mega menus and side panels from live sites.

![Navbar Gallery: headers, mega menus and banners from live sites](shot:navbar-gallery)

## 7. Texture and grain instead of gloss

**Then:** smooth gradients, drop shadows, glassy buttons.
**Now:** grain, halftones, paper textures, hand-made elements, anything that makes a site feel less generated.

You'll see it on studio and portfolio sites on [Siteinspire](/tool/siteinspire/) and [Godly](/tool/godly/).

## How to use galleries without copying

1. Name the task: "first screen for a service", "pricing page", "header with a big menu".
2. Collect 5–10 examples from two or three galleries, not one.
3. Write down what they have in common: that's the technique. Copy the technique, not the site.
